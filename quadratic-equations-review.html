Topic 1 Factoring & Finding Roots / Zeros
Key Concepts
Method 1 — Common Factor
(always check first!)
Remove the greatest common factor from every term before any other method.
Example: 2x² − 8 = 2(x² − 4) = 2(x − 2)(x + 2)
Method 2 — Inspection (when a = 1)
Find two integers that multiply to c and
add to b.
Example: x² − 7x + 12 = (x − 3)(x − 4)
because (−3)(−4) = 12 and (−3) + (−4) = −7
Method 3 — Difference of Squares (b = 0)
x² − c² = (x − c)(x + c)
Example: 4x² − 25 = (2x − 5)(2x + 5)
Requires: a) binomial, b) subtracting the two terms, c) each term is a perfect square.
Method 4 — Decomposition (when a ≠ 1)
Find two integers that multiply to ac and add to b. Split the middle term (b), then factor by grouping.
The Zero Product Law — Finding Roots
If a × b = 0, then a = 0 or b = 0. Once factored, set each factor = 0 and solve. The solutions are called roots of the equation (also called zeros of the function). Reminder: All terms must be on one side (= 0) before factoring.
Worked Example — All Four Factoring Methods with Roots Step-by-Step

Factor completely and find all roots for each equation.

(a)  Solve: x² + 4x − 21 = 0    [Inspection]
1
Check for a common factor.
No common factor among 1, 4, and −21.
2
Use inspection (a = 1): find two numbers that multiply to −21 and add to +4.
Try factors of −21: (7)(−3) = −21 and 7 + (−3) = 4 ✓
x² + 4x − 21 = (x + 7)(x − 3)
3
Apply the Zero Product Law.
(x + 7)(x − 3) = 0
x + 7 = 0    or    x − 3 = 0
x = −7        or    x = 3
The roots are x = −7 and x = 3.
(b)  Solve: 4x² − 25 = 0    [Difference of Squares]
1
Recognize the difference of squares pattern: b = 0, and both terms are perfect squares.
4x² = (2x)² and 25 = 5²
4x² − 25 = (2x − 5)(2x + 5)
2
Apply the Zero Product Law.
2x − 5 = 0   →   x = 52        2x + 5 = 0   →   x = −52
The roots are x = ±52 (i.e., 2.5 and −2.5).
(c)  Solve: 3x² − 75 = 0    [Common Factor, then Difference of Squares]
1
Remove the common factor of 3 first.
3x² − 75 = 3(x² − 25)
2
Factor the remaining expression using difference of squares.
3(x² − 25) = 3(x − 5)(x + 5)
3
Apply the Zero Product Law. (The constant factor of 3 cannot equal zero.)
x − 5 = 0   →   x = 5        x + 5 = 0   →   x = −5
The roots are x = 5 and x = −5.
(d)  Solve: 2x² − 3x − 20 = 0    [Decomposition]
1
Identify a, b, c and calculate ac.
a = 2, b = −3, c = −20.   So ac = (2)(−20) = −40.
2
Find two integers that multiply to −40 and add to −3.
Try: (−8)(5) = −40 and −8 + 5 = −3 ✓
3
Rewrite the middle term using −8 and +5, then factor by grouping.
2x² − 8x + 5x − 20
= 2x(x − 4) + 5(x − 4)
= (2x + 5)(x − 4)
4
Apply the Zero Product Law.
2x + 5 = 0   →   x = −52        x − 4 = 0   →   x = 4
The roots are x = −52 and x = 4.

Topic 2 Connecting Roots, Zeros, and x-Intercepts
Key Concepts
The Big Connection
For the function y = f(x), these three things are always the same numbers:
• The roots of the equation y = 0
• The zeros of the function f(x) = 0
• The x-intercepts of the graph of y = f(x)
Number of Zeros (X-Intercepts)
  • 2 zeros: Graph crosses the x-axis twice
  • 1 zero: Graph just touches the x-axis (vertex on axis)
  • 0 zeros: Graph does not cross the x-axis
Reading from a Graph
Zeros/Roots/x-intercepts: the x-values where the curve crosses/touches the x-axis (y = 0).
y-intercept: where the curve crosses the y-axis (substitute x = 0 into the equation, or read it directly off the graph).
Worked Example 1 — Reading a Graph to Find Zeros, y-Intercept & Equation Step-by-Step
Given the graph of y = f(x) shown below, determine:
(a) the zeros of the function    (b) the y-intercept    (c) the equation in factored form y = a(x − m)(x − n)
1
Read the zeros — where the curve crosses the x-axis (red dots).
Zeros: x = −2 and x = 4
2
Read the y-intercept — where the curve crosses the y-axis (gold dot).
y-intercept: (0, 8)
3
Write the general factored form using the zeros: zeros −2 and 4 give factors (x + 2) and (x − 4).
y = a(x + 2)(x − 4)
4
Substitute the y-intercept (0, 8) to find the value of a.
8 = a(0 + 2)(0 − 4)
8 = a(2)(−4)
8 = −8a
a = −1
Note: a = −1 confirms the parabola opens downward, which matches the graph.
Zeros: x = −2 and x = 4  |  y-intercept: (0, 8)  |  Equation: y = −(x + 2)(x − 4)
Verify with (2, 8): y = −(4)(−2) = 8 ✓
Worked Example 2 — Algebraically Finding Zeros from a Function Step-by-Step
Algebraically determine the zeros and y-intercept of f(x) = 2x² − 10x − 12. Then state the number of x-intercepts the graph has.
1
Set f(x) = 0 to find the zeros.
2x² − 10x − 12 = 0
2
Remove the common factor of 2, then divide both sides by 2.
2(x² − 5x − 6) = 0
x² − 5x − 6 = 0
3
Factor by inspection: find two numbers that multiply to −6 and add to −5.
Try: (−6)(1) = −6 and −6 + 1 = −5 ✓
(x − 6)(x + 1) = 0
x − 6 = 0    or    x + 1 = 0
x = 6         or    x = −1
4
Find the y-intercept by substituting x = 0 into the original function.
f(0) = 2(0)² − 10(0) − 12 = −12    →    y-intercept is (0, −12)
5
State the number of x-intercepts and summarize.
Two distinct zeros were found, so the graph crosses the x-axis at two points: x = −1 and x = 6.
Zeros: x = −1 and x = 6  |  y-intercept: (0, −12)  |  2 x-intercepts

Topic 3 Writing a Quadratic Equation from Zeros or x-Intercepts
Key Concepts
General Approach
If zeros (or x-intercepts) are m and n:
y = a(x − m)(x − n)
You need one additional known point to determine a.
Fractional Zeros — Use Integer Factors
x = 35 → factor: (5x − 3)
x = −12 → factor: (2x + 1)
Write the denominator as the coefficient of x.
Worked Example 1 — Finding the Equation Given Zeros and a Point Step-by-Step
A quadratic function has zeros −3 and 1, and its graph passes through (0, −6). Determine the equation.
1
Write the factored form using the zeros.
Zero −3 → factor (x + 3). Zero 1 → factor (x − 1).
y = a(x + 3)(x − 1)
2
Substitute (0, −6) to find a.
−6 = a(3)(−1) = −3a  →  a = 2
3
Write the final equations.
Factored: y = 2(x + 3)(x − 1)     General: y = 2x² + 4x − 6
y = 2(x + 3)(x − 1)    or equivalently    y = 2x² + 4x − 6
Worked Example 2 — Working with Fractional X-Intercepts Step-by-Step
A quadratic function has x-intercepts 23 and −4. Write a possible equation in the form ax² + bx + c = 0 where a, b, and c are integers.
1
Convert each x-intercept to an integer factor.
x = ⅔: the denominator is 3, so the factor is (3x − 2).    x = −4: the factor is (x + 4).
y = (3x − 2)(x + 4)
2
Expand to verify and write in general form.
(3x − 2)(x + 4) = 3x² + 12x − 2x − 8 = 3x² + 10x − 8
3
Verify the x-intercepts by setting equal to zero.
3x − 2 = 0 → x = ⅔ ✓     x + 4 = 0 → x = −4 ✓
Any multiple of this equation is also valid, e.g. 2(3x − 2)(x + 4) = 6x² + 20x − 16 = 0. The question says "a possible equation."
One possible equation: 3x² + 10x − 8 = 0

Topic 4 The Quadratic Formula
Key Concepts
For ax² + bx + c = 0  (a ≠ 0):    −b ± √(b² − 4ac) 2a
When to Use It
  • The equation cannot be factored (roots are irrational)
  • To verify roots found by factoring
  • When asked for exact values or decimals
Must be in the form ax² + bx + c = 0 first!
Number of Solutions (Discriminant)
The value of b² − 4ac tells you:
  • Positive: 2 distinct real roots
  • Zero: 1 repeated root (vertex on x-axis)
  • Negative: No real roots (no x-intercepts)
Worked Example 1 — Irrational Roots: Exact Values & Decimals Step-by-Step
Find the x-intercepts of g(x) = 3x² − 12x + 11 using the quadratic formula. Give exact values and answers to the nearest hundredth.
1
Set g(x) = 0. Identify a = 3, b = −12, c = 11.
2
Substitute into the formula.
x = −(−12) ± √((−12)² − 4(3)(11))2(3) = 12 ± √(144 − 132)6 = 12 ± √126
3
Simplify: √12 = 2√3.
x = 12 ± 2√36 = 6 ± √33     ← exact values
4
Evaluate each decimal approximation.
x ≈ 6 + 1.7323 ≈ 2.58       x ≈ 6 − 1.7323 ≈ 1.42
Exact: x = 6 + √33 and x = 6 − √33    Decimal: x ≈ 2.58 and x ≈ 1.42
Worked Example 2 — Rearranging First, Then Applying the Formula Step-by-Step
Determine the roots of 3x² = 7x + 6 using the quadratic formula. Give answers as exact values and to the nearest tenth.
1
Rearrange so all terms are on one side (= 0).
Subtract 7x and 6 from both sides.
3x² − 7x − 6 = 0
2
Identify a = 3, b = −7, c = −6 and substitute.
x = −(−7) ± √((−7)² − 4(3)(−6))2(3) = 7 ± √(49 + 72)6 = 7 ± √1216
3
√121 = 11 — simplifies to rational roots (could have been factored!).
x = 7 + 116 = 3       x = 7 − 116 = −23
When the discriminant is a perfect square, the roots are rational — the equation could have been factored by decomposition: (3x + 2)(x − 3) = 0.
Roots: x = 3 and x = −23 ≈ −0.7

Topic 5 Applications of Quadratic Equations
Key Concepts
Problem-Solving Steps
  1. Define a variable for the unknown quantity.
  2. Write a quadratic equation from the given information.
  3. Solve by factoring or the quadratic formula.
  4. State the answer in context — reject any values that don't make sense (e.g., negative lengths or times).
Worked Example 1 — Height / Projectile Motion Step-by-Step
A stone is thrown vertically upward at 20 m/s. Its height, in metres, after t seconds is h(t) = 20t − 5t².
(a) Write and solve an equation to find when the stone is 15 m above the ground.
(b) Explain what the two solutions represent.
a
Set h(t) = 15 and rearrange to standard form.
20t − 5t² = 15  →  5t² − 20t + 15 = 0  →  t² − 4t + 3 = 0
2
Factor and apply the Zero Product Law.
(t − 1)(t − 3) = 0  →  t = 1 s  or  t = 3 s
b
Interpret both solutions in context.
Both are positive, so both are valid. The stone reaches 15 m on the way up (t = 1 s) and again on the way down (t = 3 s).
The stone is 15 m above the ground at t = 1 second and t = 3 seconds.
Worked Example 2 — Area of a Rectangle Step-by-Step
The area of a rectangular field, in square metres, is x² + 12x + 35.
(a) Factor the expression to find the length and width as binomials.
(b) Determine the actual dimensions when x = 8.
a
Factor by inspection: find two numbers multiplying to 35, adding to 12.
(5)(7) = 35 and 5 + 7 = 12 ✓
x² + 12x + 35 = (x + 5)(x + 7)
So length = (x + 7) metres and width = (x + 5) metres.
b
Substitute x = 8.
Length = 8 + 7 = 15 m     Width = 8 + 5 = 13 m
Check: 15 × 13 = 195 m²  =  8² + 12(8) + 35 = 64 + 96 + 35 = 195 ✓
(a) Length = (x + 7) m, Width = (x + 5) m     (b) 15 m × 13 m
Worked Example 3 — Setting Up a Quadratic from a Word Problem Step-by-Step
Two positive integers have a product of 54 and their sum is 15. Determine the two integers by setting up and solving a quadratic equation.
1
Define variables and write two equations.
Let the integers be x and y. Then: x + y = 15 and xy = 54.
From the sum: y = 15 − x
2
Substitute into the product equation.
x(15 − x) = 54  →  15x − x² = 54  →  x² − 15x + 54 = 0
3
Factor and solve.
Find two numbers multiplying to 54, adding to −15: (−6)(−9) ✓
(x − 6)(x − 9) = 0  →  x = 6  or  x = 9
4
State both integers.
If x = 6 then y = 15 − 6 = 9. If x = 9 then y = 15 − 9 = 6. Same pair!
The two integers are 6 and 9.   Verify: 6 + 9 = 15 ✓ and 6 × 9 = 54 ✓
